RNA Beads to Cells

RNA Beads to Cells Analysis Explanation

  • RNA Barcode Rank Plot (left): In the library, transcripts from the same cell are grouped under a unified cell ID, each tagged with unique molecular identifiers (UMIs). However, RNA from ambient sources or dying cells may also be captured during library preparation. This barcode rank plot visualizes the distribution of total UMI counts per cell ID (x-axis: cell ID rank; y-axis: UMI count, both in log scale). Cell IDs with higher UMI counts (on the left) are more likely to represent real cells, while lower-count IDs (on the right) are often background. Blue points indicate high-confidence cells, gray points represent background (e.g., empty droplets), and the blue gradient region marks a transitional zone identified by an ambient RNA model. Hovering over each point reveals its rank, UMI count, and the percentage of real cells in its neighborhood, aiding in the distinction between true cells and noise.
  • Bead Count Distribution (right): This plot displays the distribution of barcoded bead counts per droplet among identified real cells. While the number of beads per droplet is theoretically expected to follow a Poisson distribution, deviations may occur due to factors such as low RNA capture or inefficient bead merging. The distribution is affected by cell filtering thresholds and dynamically updates accordingly. If most droplets contain only one bead, it is advisable to check whether the oligo library has sufficient sequencing depth (recommended >50M reads) and whether it is properly matched to the cDNA library, as these factors can impact overall data quality and cell recovery.

Targeting

ATAC Targeting Analysis Explanation

  • TSS Enrichment Profile (left): This plot displays the aggregate chromatin accessibility signal—measured as the number of cut sites per base—within a ±1,000 bp window around annotated transcription start sites (TSS). The signal is normalized to the minimum value within the window for each TSS. A sharp peak centered at the TSS indicates well-preserved nuclear integrity and high data quality. In contrast, a flat or noisy profile may suggest sample degradation, inefficient lysis, or high background contamination.
  • TSS Targeting Plot (right): This scatter plot visualizes the relationship between chromatin accessibility and transcription start site enrichment for each barcode. The x-axis shows the total number of fragments per barcode, while the y-axis represents the fraction of fragments overlapping the ±2 kb region around TSSs. Ideally, high-quality cells cluster in the upper-right quadrant (high fragment count and high TSS enrichment), while background barcodes appear in the lower-left. Colored points distinguish between cell-associated and background barcodes, helping identify real cells and assess library quality. Poor separation or broad distribution may indicate issues such as over-lysis, suboptimal library prep, or elevated background noise.

Mapping Metrics

Mapping Metrics Explanation

  • All Reads: Metrics calculated from all raw sequenced reads, including reads from cells, background RNA, and empty droplets.
  • Filtered Cells: Metrics calculated from reads assigned to high-confidence cells identified through cell calling.
  • Reads mapped to genome: The proportion of sequencing reads aligned to any position in the reference genome, including both uniquely mapped and multi-mapped reads. This metric reflects overall alignment efficiency. Lower values may indicate poor sample quality, mismatches with the reference genome, or sequencing errors.
  • Reads mapped confidently to genome: The proportion of reads reliably aligned to the reference genome, primarily consisting of uniquely mapped reads. Multi-mapped reads are retained if they align to a single exonic locus alongside one or more non-exonic loci. This metric indicates the reliability and biological relevance of read placement. Lower proportions may result from poor sequencing quality or mismatches between the sample and the reference genome.
  • Reads mapped confidently to transcriptome: The proportion of reads uniquely mapped to the transcriptome and assigned to a single gene. For exonic reads, alignment must align with annotated splice junctions (intronic reads are excluded). These reads directly contribute to UMI counting and are a critical indicator of library quality. Higher values indicate greater specificity and mRNA capture reliability; lower values may reflect incomplete transcriptome annotations or poor sample quality.
  • Reads mapped to mitochondrial genome: Percentage of reads aligned to the mitochondrial genome. High values may indicate mitochondrial RNA enrichment or stressed/damaged cells.
  • Reads mapped confidently to exonic regions: The proportion of reads confidently aligned to annotated exonic regions. A read is classified as exonic if at least 50% of its sequence overlaps with an exon.
  • Reads mapped confidently to intronic regions: The proportion of reads confidently aligned to annotated intronic regions. Reads are classified as intronic if they do not meet exonic criteria but intersect with annotated introns. These reads often originate from unspliced pre-mRNA or nuclear RNA.
  • Reads mapped confidently to intergenic regions: The proportion of reads confidently aligned to genomic regions not annotated as part of any gene (intergenic). Reads are classified as intergenic if they meet neither exonic nor intronic criteria. Elevated values may suggest non-specific amplification during library preparation or incomplete reference annotation.
  • Reads mapped antisense to gene: The proportion of reads confidently mapped to the transcriptome but aligned in the antisense orientation relative to annotated genes. These may arise from technical noise, natural antisense transcripts, or insufficient strand specificity during library preparation.

Cell Quality Metrics

Cell Quality Metrics Explanation

  • Percent of duplicates: This metric quantifies the proportion of sequenced read pairs, associated with cell barcodes, that are identified as PCR duplicates—defined as reads mapping to identical genomic coordinates. A higher duplication rate (typically ≥20%) is indicative of elevated sequencing saturation, reflecting that many accessible regions have already been captured and sequenced multiple times. This value is influenced by both library complexity and sequencing depth.
  • Jaccard threshold: The Jaccard threshold represents the minimum similarity score required to merge multiple bead barcodes from the same droplet into a single cell identity. The Jaccard index quantifies the overlap between two barcodes based on their fragment sets, calculated as the ratio of intersecting fragments to the union of both sets. This threshold is automatically determined using the Otsu algorithm to distinguish genuine droplet co-occupancy from unrelated barcode pairs. A minimum value of 0.025 is applied to maintain analysis robustness in low-saturation libraries. This threshold is critical for accurate bead collapsing and reliable single-cell chromatin profiling.
  • (Left) Fragment Saturation Curve: This plot illustrates the median number of unique (non-duplicated) fragments detected per cell across a range of downsampled sequencing depths. The x-axis shows the average number of read pairs per cell after downsampling, while the y-axis represents the corresponding median number of unique fragments per cell. In general, the curve rises steeply at low sequencing depths and gradually plateaus, reflecting the point at which most accessible chromatin regions have been sufficiently captured. This visualization helps assess whether additional sequencing would provide significant improvement in data complexity.
  • (Right) Barcode Pairwise Jaccard Index Distribution: This plot illustrates the ranked distribution of Jaccard similarity scores across all bead barcode pairs, which are used to determine whether multiple beads should be merged into a single cell identity. The x-axis shows barcode pair rankings ordered by decreasing similarity, while the y-axis displays the corresponding Jaccard index on a log scale. Higher Jaccard values (blue points) suggest that the barcode pairs originate from the same droplet and are therefore candidates for merging. Lower values (gray points) reflect unrelated barcodes that should remain distinct. The curve's inflection point marks a transition between bead pairs to be merged and those to be excluded, and helps define the optimal merging threshold.
